Core Standard: Great State. Great Service. Great Results. Applies to AXU-II, YGLC, SG & future units.

Part I — Foundation & Operating Philosophy

1.1 Our Core Philosophy

We don't just solve problems. We move customers from their current state to a Great State. Our responsibility does not end when a transaction is completed or a service is delivered. Our responsibility is to design, deliver and verify customer value.

1.2 The Great State Definition

A Great State is the measurable improvement between a customer's initial situation and their desired outcome. Every customer engagement must identify:

  1. Current State: Where is the customer now?
  2. Desired State: Where does the customer want to go?
  3. Gap: What prevents the customer from reaching that state?
  4. Solution: What intervention can close the gap?
  5. Outcome: What measurable improvement has occurred?
Fundamental Rule: Never design a solution before understanding the customer's actual problem.

1.3 The Seven Operating Principles

  • 1. Customer Understanding Before Selling: Diagnose before offering solutions.
  • 2. Evidence Before Assumptions: Separate facts from unverified claims.
  • 3. Outcome Before Activity: Value is defined by result, not output volume.
  • 4. Quality Before Speed: Deliver excellence without unnecessary delay.
  • 5. Clear Communication: Transparency at every touchpoint.
  • 6. Consistency Through Systems: Rely on standardized SOP execution.
  • 7. Continuous Improvement: Learn and adapt systematically from operational evidence.

1.4 The Customer Experience Lifecycle

Discovery → First Contact → Research → Diagnosis → Solution Design → Purchase → Service Delivery → Quality Verification → Customer Success → Retention → Continuous Improvement.

Lifecycle Standards: Every stage must have an assigned owner, clear trigger, defined procedure, quality standard, expected output, verification method, and failure recovery process.

SOP 01 — CUSTOMER RESEARCH Research

1. Purpose

Understand the customer deeply enough to identify what they actually need, value, fear, expect and are willing to pay for. Distinguish between what customers say, do, and actually buy.

2. Trigger

New product launch, entering new segments, declining conversion, repeated complaints, periodic intelligence reviews.

3. Required Inputs

Interviews, sales logs, feedback, purchase history, support records, market observations, web analytics.

4. Standard Procedure

  • Step 1 — Define Objective: Write one specific question with a decision attached.
  • Step 2 — Identify Segments: Group by needs, problems, outcomes, budget, and use cases.
  • Step 3 — Collect First-Hand Evidence: Conduct interviews; ask about past behavior rather than hypotheticals.
  • Step 4 — Analyze Behavior: Classify into Pain Points, Desires, Objections, Buying Triggers.
  • Step 5 — Build Evidence Database: Separate Facts, Assumptions, and Unknowns.
  • Step 6 — Identify Patterns: Look for repeated insights across multiple customers.
  • Step 7 — Produce Recommendations: Translate findings into product, pricing, and offer decisions.

5. Quality Standards & Output

Traceable sources; non-hypothetical proof; deliver Customer Research Report, Persona Profiles, Pain/Desire Register.

6. KPIs & Failure Protocol

KPIs: Meaningful conversations, validated problems, decision conversion. Failure Protocol: Stop treating assumptions as facts; conduct additional target interviews.

SOP 02 — CUSTOMER DIAGNOSIS Diagnosis

1. Purpose

Identify the customer's real problem and define their Desired Great State before proposing a solution.

2. Trigger

Inquiry, consultation request, personalized purchase, dissatisfaction report, custom support request.

3. Standard Procedure

  • Step 1 — Establish Objective: Translate vague requests into specific outcomes.
  • Step 2 — Identify Current State: Document abilities, workflows, previous failed attempts, and constraints.
  • Step 3 — Define Desired Great State: Specify expected result, timeline, and measurable metrics.
  • Step 4 — Identify Gap: Classify obstacles into knowledge, skill, resource, or process barriers.
  • Step 5 — Identify Root Causes: Uncover actual underlying causes, not surface symptoms.
  • Step 6 — Assess Feasibility: Confirm capability, timeline, budget, and risk factors.
  • Step 7 — Confirm Diagnosis: Summarize and obtain customer sign-off before proceeding.

4. Outputs & Failure Protocol

Diagnosis Report, Current/Desired State Profiles. If unclear: pause solution design and collect targeted follow-up data.

SOP 03 — SOLUTION DESIGN Architecture

1. Purpose

Design the most appropriate solution to move the customer from Current State to Desired Great State.

2. Standard Procedure

  • Step 1 — Generate Candidates: Draft multiple viable intervention paths.
  • Step 2 — Evaluate Candidates: Score by value, probability, effort, business cost, margin, risk.
  • Step 3 — Select Best Solution: Choose path with highest outcome probability and sound economics.
  • Step 4 — Design Delivery Plan: Define sequence, milestones, responsibilities, and timeline.
  • Step 5 — Design Experience: Ensure ease of use, clear onboarding, and minimal friction.
  • Step 6 — Identify Failure Scenarios: Map out contingencies for delays or non-cooperation.
  • Step 7 — Present Solution: Explain deliverable, price, conditions, and obtain formal approval.
SOP 04 — SERVICE DELIVERY Fulfillment

1. Purpose

Deliver promised solution consistently, accurately, and within agreed conditions.

2. Steps

Confirm Order → Complete Onboarding → Prepare Delivery → Execute Service → Maintain Logs → Intermediate Check → Complete Delivery → Handover.

3. Quality Standards

On-time execution; zero undocumented changes; immediate escalation of delays to customer.

SOP 05 — COMMUNICATION Interaction

1. Purpose

Make every customer interaction clear, timely, professional, and trustworthy.

2. Standard 5-Step Structure

1. Acknowledge Request → 2. Explain Relevant Info → 3. State Next Action → 4. Provide Timeline → 5. Confirm Next Steps.

SOP 06 — QUALITY CONTROL Verification

1. Purpose

Ensure every product or deliverable meets defined standards prior to customer release.

2. Defect Classification

  • Critical: Blocks safe or core functional use (Release forbidden).
  • Major: Materially affects outcome or experience.
  • Minor: Aesthetic or non-material flaw requiring documentation.
SOP 07 — PROBLEM RECOVERY Recovery

1. Purpose

Recover trust and resolve failures through fast, fair, and accountable action.

2. Recovery Protocol

1. Receive & Acknowledge → 2. Assess Severity → 3. Contain Damage → 4. Root Cause Analysis → 5. Honest Comm → 6. Execute Recovery → 7. Confirm Resolution.

SOP 08 — CUSTOMER SUCCESS Outcome

1. Purpose

Verify that customers achieve real outcomes rather than simply receiving a product or service.

2. Procedure

Baseline Recording → Metric Definition → Success Plan → Progress Monitoring → Barrier Removal → Outcome Verification.

SOP 09 — RETENTION & RELATIONSHIP Retention

1. Purpose

Build durable relationships through earned value, trust, and continuous relevance—never manipulation.

2. Principles

Clear cancellation policies; zero forced upselling; regular relationship value reviews.

SOP 10 — CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT Evolution

1. Purpose

Convert feedback, failures, and operational data into standardized SOP updates.

2. Procedure

Collect Evidence → Identify Improvement → Prioritize Impact → Root Cause Analysis → Controlled Testing → Standardize Version Control.

Part III — Master Operating Control

3.1 Universal SOP Structure

Every task-level SOP must define: SOP ID, Purpose, Scope, Trigger, RACI Owners, Inputs, Procedure Steps, Quality Standards, Outputs, Verification Checklist, KPIs, Failure Protocols, and Version Control.

3.2 RACI Responsibility Matrix

RoleDefinitionPrinciple
R — ResponsiblePerforms the execution steps.Does the actual work.
A — AccountableOwns final result & decision.Exactly ONE Accountable owner.
C — ConsultedProvides expert input/data.Two-way prior communication.
I — InformedUpdated on status/outcome.One-way post notification.

3.3 Escalation Matrix

Level 1 — Standard Operations

Handled directly by assigned Service Owner within normal SOP boundaries.

Level 2 — Operational Exceptions

Escalated to Operations Supervisor when steps deviate or minor delays occur.

Level 3 — Critical Incidents

Escalated immediately to Founder/Authorized Decision Maker (privacy breaches, financial errors, major outages).

3.4 Universal Definition of Done

A task is DONE only when: Procedure followed + Output exists + Quality check passed + System updated + Customer result verified + Next owner assigned.
